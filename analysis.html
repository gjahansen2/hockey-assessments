This dashboard allows users to examine assessment scores and their associated uncertainty using either example assessment scores or real, anonymized data uploaded by the user. Scores stay in this browser.

Changing the player group automatically loads and analyzes the corresponding example scores. Upload your file again to examine real data for that group.

1. Enter anonymized scores

Use player,goalie,session,evaluator,score, with Y for goalies and N for skaters. Each row is one integer rating from 1–7. Keep evaluator IDs consistent across sessions; omit unobserved ratings. One file can contain both groups.

2. Set session weights and estimate uncertainty

Players are evaluated across several sessions that demonstrate different types of skills. Users may choose to give scores from certain sessions more or less weight. By default, all sessions are weighted equally.

To increase emphasis on a session, increase its weight. A weight of 1.1, with all others set to 1, gives that session 10% more influence than each other session in the weighted mean. To reduce emphasis, choose a weight between 0 and 1. A weight of 0.5 gives that session half as much influence as a session weighted 1; a weight of 0 excludes it.

Weights also affect uncertainty and recommendations: higher weights represent greater confidence in a session’s scores. Players who were not evaluated in a session have no score from it included in their weighted mean. Changing its weight does not directly change their mean, although their ranking and boundary probabilities can change as the evidence for other players changes.

How session weights work

Weights express relative confidence in sessions, rather than extra evaluations. Positive weights are normalized to average 1, so changing every weight from 1 to 2 has no effect. Within the model, both session-performance SD and independent rating-error SD are divided by the square root of the normalized weight. Persistent evaluator effects are retained. This is an explicit reliability assumption: weights should reflect how informative sessions are, not simply which result is preferred.

Uncertainty assumptions

These SDs are assumptions, not error estimates established by this upload. Try reasonable alternatives to assess how sensitive conclusions are.

Model details and limitations

The ordered-probit model treats integer ratings as seven categories; no rounding occurs. It estimates skill, session performance, persistent evaluator scoring offsets and widths, and evaluator–player impressions. Skills have Normal(4, 1.7) priors bounded to 1–7. Evaluator offsets have Normal(0, 1) priors; widths have lognormal priors with untruncated mean 1 and SD 0.5, bounded to 0.5–1.75. The six anchored thresholds are −2.5, −1.5, −0.5, 0.5, 1.5, 2.5.

Four seeded MCMC chains retain 8,000 or 16,000 draws. Split-chain R̂ and approximate ESS assess mixing for skills and ranks, plus the goalie skill difference. Passing sampling checks does not validate the model. Skater probabilities concern reference rank boundaries across the complete uploaded pool. They do not reenact staged cuts, score breaks, positional requirements, or exact final team assignments. Group-specific competition effects, selective recording, and broader team fit require further consideration. The 25–75% rule is an adjustable review policy.

Session weights add a reliability assumption to the existing model. A higher weight changes the assumed observation and session-performance variances, rather than replicating scores. Relative weights and the nuisance SDs jointly influence probabilities, so sensitivity checks matter.
